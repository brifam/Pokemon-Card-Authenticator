Tips

  • Crop Tight: Frame the photo so only card is visible, leave no background space or srface textures around the edges.
  • Lighting: Use bright, even light to avoid heavy shadows, glare, or reflections on card surface.
  • Focus and Angle: Hold the camera directly above the card so it lies flat and straight, making sure all text and borders are sharp.
  • Oreintation: Use a clear, back-facing photo of the Pokémon card back to match the model's training set

The Mission

In second grade, I remember walking through a flea market and buying my first ever pack of Pokémon cards. When I proudly brought them to school the next day, my friends broke the news — they were fake. I was devastated.

Fake Pokémon cards have been around almost as long as the real ones, but with the recent surge in collecting, new collectors are more vulnerable than ever to being misled into buying counterfeits. Seeing this happen to others, and remembering that moment back in second grade, is what inspired me to build PokéCheck AI — a website that helps people tell whether their Pokémon cards are real or fake.

How it works

PokéCheck AI uses a custom-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) built in PyTorch to classify Pokémon trading cards as real or fake. The system processes uploaded images through a standardized computer vision pipeline, applying normalization, resizing, and feature extraction to analyze subtle visual patterns such as color gradients, font rendering, and border alignments. By evaluating these high-level feature maps against patterns learned during model training, PokéCheck AI calculates a real-versus-fake probability score and returns the prediction alongside a statistical confidence percentage in real time.

Key Findings

  • Optimization Bottleneck (SGD): Initial training using standard SGD with a high learning rate (0.1) plateaued at 71.21% validation accuracy by overshooting and predicting only the majority class. Lowering the learning rate (0.001) prevented overshooting but stalled progress entirely at 57.86% accuracy.

  • Rapid Convergence with Adam: Switching the optimizer to Adam (LR = 0.001) resolved convergence issues immediately, achieving 97.50% training accuracy and 95.93% validation accuracy in just 15 epochs.

  • Deployed Weights: Experiment 3 produced the optimal model parameters, which were exported to models/pokemon_card_model_v1.pth for live inference.

Note on Accuracy

Predictions are based purely on 2D visual feature analysis (color, borders, and print layout). Computer vision cannot evaluate key physical authenticity factors such as card stock texture, holo foil depth, weight, or light transparency (the "flashlight test"). For high-value transactions, always pair digital checks with physical inspection or professional grading.
